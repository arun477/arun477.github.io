Robots that learn hands-on work from people

Startups are paying people to wear cameras, sensor gloves and touch-sensing hardware so robots can learn real hands-on work, from lab chemistry to household chores. The bet: recorded human skill, not more internet text, is what physical AI is short of.

  1. New this week$215M led by Lightspeed, with IVP, Premji Invest and Lux CapitalSpotted 23 Oct 2026ThemeWarp SciencesUS chemistry lab for drug research where chemists wearing cameras and sensor gloves train robots to make and test molecules.
  2. New this week$30M seed led by Peak XVreportedSpotted 23 Oct 2026MaxinsightsFirst-person video of people doing everyday tasks, sold as training data for robot foundation models.
  3. New this week$30M pre-seed led by redalpine, Notion Capital and firstminute capitalreportedSpotted 23 Oct 2026Enact IntelligenceRobot actuators that sense force and touch, so robot hands can do dexterous work.

Checking, securing and insuring agents
